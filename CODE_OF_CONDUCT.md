# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume the other person is trying to help.

## What is expected

- Talk about the code, not the person who wrote it. "This drops messages when
  the mailbox is empty" is a bug report; "this is sloppy work" is not.
- Take a disagreement about a technical decision as a disagreement about a
  technical decision. Most of them are settled by a measurement.
- Accept that a maintainer may say no to a change that works. Scope is a real
  constraint and so is the cost of keeping something alive.
- Remember that bug reports about an email sorter often start with somebody's
  actual mail. Do not ask anyone to paste more of it than you need, and do not
  repeat what they did paste.

## What is not

Harassment, insults, or personal attacks. Sexual attention or imagery.
Publishing anyone's private information. Sustained disruption of a discussion.
Anything you would not say to the person if you were sitting beside them.

None of this depends on who somebody is: their experience, their nationality,
their gender or gender identity, their sexuality, their religion, their age,
their disability, or how they look.

## If something goes wrong

Report it privately by opening a
[security advisory](https://github.com/NickCysEDU/Mail-Manager/security/advisories/new),
which is the only private channel this repository has. Include what happened
and where. It will be read.

A maintainer may edit, hide or delete a comment, close a thread, or block an
account, and will say that they have done so and why. Repeated or deliberate
breaches mean losing access to the project.

## Where this applies

Anywhere the project is: issues, pull requests, commits, discussions, and
anywhere somebody is representing it elsewhere.
